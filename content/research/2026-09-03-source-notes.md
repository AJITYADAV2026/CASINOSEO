## Source 1 — Hokkaido Business Groups Push for 2027 IR Application

**Source:** GGRAsia, “Hokkaido business groups to urge Japanese prefecture to meet 2027 IR application window,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/hokkaido-business-groups-to-urge-japanese-prefecture-to-meet-2027-ir-application-window

Eight Hokkaido business organisations planned to submit a formal request on 8 September asking governor Naomichi Suzuki to accelerate work on a casino integrated-resort proposal so the prefecture could meet Japan’s second-round application window, scheduled for 6 May to 5 November 2027.

The Hokkaido Economic Federation was among the organisations behind the request. Hokkaido had not entered the first national application round, but three overseas casino operators and 12 domestic companies had recently responded to the prefecture’s market-interest questionnaire.

No Hokkaido project is approved. Japan’s only approved integrated resort remains the JPY1.51 trillion MGM Osaka development, scheduled to open at the end of 2030. The final digest must therefore describe Hokkaido’s position as a local business advocacy effort and early-stage policy process.

## Source 2 — Grand Korea Leisure August Sales Decline

**Source:** GGRAsia, “GKL’s August casino sales down year-on-year and sequentially to US$23mln,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/gkls-august-casino-sales-down-year-on-year-and-sequentially-to-us23mln

Grand Korea Leisure reported August casino sales of nearly KRW31.11 billion, approximately US$22.91 million. Sales were down 11.3% year on year and 25.4% month on month.

Aggregate casino sales for the first eight months of 2026 reached almost KRW299.97 billion, 5.6% above the comparable prior-year period. Seoul Dragon City generated KRW12.85 billion in August, down about 10.0% year on year and 2.0% sequentially. Seoul Gangnam generated nearly KRW12.41 billion, down 13.3% year on year and 43.1% from July.

The GKL figures provide a useful counterpoint to Paradise Co’s record August result published the previous day, underscoring divergent operator performance within South Korea’s foreigner-only casino segment.

## Source 3 — Kangwon Land Links Resort Strategy to Regional Tourism

**Source:** GGRAsia, “Kangwon Land Inc’s new CEO pledges to make resort part of a regional tourism offering,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/kangwon-land-incs-new-ceo-pledges-to-make-resort-part-of-a-regional-tourism-offering

Newly appointed chief executive Kim Do-gyun said Kangwon Land would accelerate plans to position High1 Resort as part of a broader tourism offer connected with forests and coal-mining heritage attractions in the surrounding former mining region.

The company’s K-HIT refresh and development programme has previously been described as involving KRW3.00 trillion, approximately US$2.21 billion. Kim also said Kangwon Land would pursue regulatory reform as it seeks to develop into a “global public integrated resort.”

Kangwon Land is the only South Korean operator permitted to offer casino gambling to local citizens. Under the Special Act supporting abandoned mine areas, it contributes up to 13% of annual casino revenue to an Abandoned Mine Fund. The strategy should be presented as management direction and an ongoing development programme, not as evidence that every proposed investment is newly approved.

## Source 4 — EGT Receives UAE Gaming-Vendor Licence

**Source:** GGRAsia, “Euro Games Technology gets gaming vendor licence in UAE,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/euro-games-technology-gets-gaming-vendor-licence-in-uae

Euro Games Technology stated that the United Arab Emirates’ General Commercial Gaming Regulatory Authority had licensed the company to supply its land-based gaming-product portfolio to licensed operators in the country.

The approval concerns supplier activity and does not itself create a consumer casino or authorize unlicensed gaming operations. EGT described the UAE framework as placing emphasis on compliance, integrity, and responsible industry development.

## Source 5 — Newport World Resorts and Tourism Department Entertainment Partnership

**Source:** GGRAsia, “Newport World Resorts, Philippines tourism department team up for entertainment offerings,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/newport-world-resorts-philippines-tourism-department-team-up-for-entertainment-offerings

The Philippine Department of Tourism signed a memorandum of understanding with Newport World Resorts to promote entertainment-led tourism through live performance, cultural productions, culinary experiences, and other leisure attractions.

The department planned to include Newport offerings in the “Love the Philippines” and “Discover More to Love” campaigns, while the resort would develop “stay, dine and watch” packages, Metro Manila itineraries, and potential promotional ticket or accommodation rates.

Although Newport is a casino and leisure complex, the announced partnership was framed around entertainment, culture, food, and tourism rather than gambling promotion. The final digest should preserve that distinction.

## Source 6 — Macau Operators Expected to Maintain Elevated Promotion

**Source:** GGRAsia, “Macau casino promotional spend to remain elevated in 2H: CBRE,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://www.ggrasia.com/macau-casino-promotional-spend-to-remain-elevated-in-2h-cbre

CBRE estimated that market-wide casino commissions declined sequentially to US$1.57 billion in the second quarter of 2026 from US$1.65 billion in the first quarter. The brokerage forecast commissions of approximately US$1.62 billion in the third quarter and US$1.73 billion in the fourth quarter, equivalent to around 20.5% and 20.6% of gross gaming revenue respectively.

CBRE described Macau as a highly competitive promotional environment and said elevated customer reinvestment and higher operating costs continued to pressure concessionaire margins. The brokerage also expected mid-single-digit year-on-year September revenue growth against an easier comparison base.

These are analyst estimates and should not be presented as realised operator spending or guaranteed market outcomes.

## Source 7 — Virginia Responsible-Gambling Roundtable

**Source:** Virginia Mercury, Markus Schmidt, “As sports betting surges, Virginia weighs how to curb gambling harms,” published 3 September 2026.

**URL:** https://virginiamercury.com/2026/09/03/as-sports-betting-surges-virginia-weighs-how-to-curb-gambling-harms/

Virginia lawmakers, public-health specialists, and gambling-industry representatives met in Richmond on 2 September for a Responsible Gaming Roundtable focused on customer-control tools, youth education, treatment access, and safeguards for possible future online-casino legislation.

Virginia was reported to have five operating casinos and 11 approved sports-betting permit holders. Casinos generated more than US$102 million in adjusted gaming revenue in July 2026, while June sports wagering exceeded US$589 million, according to linked state data.

The report cited the National Council on Problem Gambling’s 2024 national survey, which found that 8% of American adults frequently experienced at least one indicator of problematic gambling. The cited rates were 17% among traditional sports bettors and 24% among fantasy-sports players, with adults aged 18–34 facing higher risk.

Virginia’s statewide support network includes treatment, peer recovery, support groups, financial counselling, provider training, and prevention work. The article also noted that 2026 online-casino bills passed different versions in the two legislative chambers but were not reconciled before adjournment; the issue was expected to return in 2027. This was a policy discussion and legislative outlook, not an enacted online-casino law.
