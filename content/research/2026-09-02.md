# CasinoVerse Daily Research Digest — 2 September 2026

The principal casino-industry developments published on **2 September 2026** centered on contrasting Asian market signals and a consequential United States regulatory dispute. Macau analysts expected year-on-year revenue growth to resume in September after a softer August; South Korea’s Paradise Co reported record monthly casino revenue; gaming supplier RGB outlined machine-replacement and digital-platform opportunities; Philippine tourism officials discussed extending visa-free access to Clark and other destinations; and New Jersey asked the U.S. Supreme Court to address whether state gambling rules apply to sports-event prediction markets.[1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

> **Research window:** This digest covers stories whose publishers visibly dated them **2 September 2026**. It is an editorial research summary, not gambling, legal, or investment advice.

## Lead Story: Macau Revenue Expected to Rebound in September

Separate forecasts from JP Morgan Securities and Seaport Research Partners pointed to renewed year-on-year growth in Macau casino gross gaming revenue during September. JP Morgan projected MOP19.64 billion, approximately **7% higher year on year**, while Seaport projected around MOP20.12 billion, or **10% growth**.[1]

The outlook followed August market-wide GGR of MOP21.89 billion, which was **1.2% lower year on year** but **8.1% higher than July**. JP Morgan maintained a full-year 2026 Macau GGR forecast of MOP254.28 billion, representing 3% annual growth.[1]

The important qualification is that September’s expected expansion partly reflects an easier comparison with September 2025, when the market was affected by a typhoon. The forecasts therefore indicate potential year-on-year improvement without necessarily implying a stronger sequential month.[1]

## South Korea: Paradise Co Reports Record Monthly Casino Revenue

Paradise Co reported August casino revenue of KRW104.91 billion, approximately US$76.8–76.9 million. That was **32.1% higher year on year** and **35.8% higher than July**, with growth across the operator’s four foreigner-only casino venues.[2] [3]

Table-game revenue reached KRW99.43 billion, up 32.7% year on year, while machine revenue rose 23.1% to KRW5.47 billion. Paradise City in Incheon was the largest contributor, generating nearly KRW58.79 billion.[2]

Independent coverage added an important operational detail: VIP visiting days were almost unchanged from a year earlier, but table hold increased to 14.6% from 11.4%. The stronger revenue result therefore coincided with a higher hold rate rather than a comparable rise in VIP visits.[3]

## United States: Prediction Markets Face a Supreme Court Test

New Jersey announced a petition asking the U.S. Supreme Court to review whether states may enforce their gambling regulations against sports-event prediction markets. The Attorney General’s office argued that a divided Third Circuit decision threatened state authority and could disrupt the established relationship between federal derivatives oversight and state-regulated sports wagering.[4]

The state framed the dispute around consumer and public-interest protections, including safeguards for minors, problem-gambling mitigation, financial stability, and the prevention of insider trading. Because this is an official statement from a litigating party, its claims should be read as **New Jersey’s legal position**, not as a forecast of the Court’s eventual decision.[4]

Trade reporting published the same day showed why the dispute matters commercially. Professional market makers increasingly provide pricing and liquidity on prediction exchanges. White Swan Data founder Bernard Marantelli said his company represented as much as 40% of activity on some secondary platforms and estimated that NFL prediction markets could produce US$5 billion–US$7 billion of liability in a single week. Those figures are industry-participant estimates, not audited market totals.[5]

## Supplier Outlook: RGB Targets Machine Replacements and Digital Services

Gaming supplier RGB said it was targeting delivery of the remaining 1,700 machines under a 3,000-unit 2026 sales goal. Management identified potential demand from Philippine integrated resorts and from a Macau replacement cycle associated with updated electronic-gaming-machine technical standards.[6]

The supplier had submitted a tender to a major Macau casino and expected a decision in the third quarter, with possible delivery in the fourth quarter. RGB also described discussions with Philippine casino resorts about game content, aggregation, and managed white-label services, although the relevant bids and negotiations had not been completed.[6]

RGB’s second-quarter shareholder profit had fallen 26.3% year on year to MYR10.3 million even as revenue increased 15.5% to MYR109.6 million, illustrating the margin pressure behind management’s second-half growth priorities.[6]

## Casino Culture & Travel: Philippines Considers Wider Visa-Free Access

Philippine tourism secretary Dita Angara-Mathay said the government was working to extend visa-free entry for Chinese-mainland passport holders beyond Cebu and Manila to **Bohol, Palawan, Clark, and Caticlan**. The proposal still required broader government approval.[7]

The Department of Tourism reported 4.11 million foreign arrivals in the first eight months of 2026, up 3.7% year on year. Chinese arrivals increased nearly 69.2%, while Indian arrivals grew 32.2%. Clark’s position as an airport, tourism hub, and casino-resort market gives the proposal direct relevance to integrated-resort visitation.[7]

## What the Day’s Coverage Suggests

The 2 September news cycle showed that casino-industry growth was not uniform. Macau’s September outlook depended partly on comparison effects; Paradise Co’s record result was supported by a stronger hold rate; RGB’s expansion plans remained dependent on tenders and developing digital agreements; and Philippine visitation policy remained at proposal stage.[1] [2] [3] [6] [7]

At the same time, the New Jersey petition demonstrated that prediction markets had become a material issue for the regulated gambling sector. The dispute is no longer only about product labels: it concerns which level of government sets consumer-protection rules and how exchange-style sports contracts coexist with state-licensed wagering.[4] [5]
