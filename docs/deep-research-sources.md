# CasinoVerse Deep Research Source Notes

## Public Health and Responsible Entertainment

The World Health Organization defines gambling as risking money or another item of value on an uncertain event and states that harms can include financial stress, relationship breakdown, family violence, mental illness, and suicide. Its 2 December 2024 fact sheet estimates that 1.2% of the global adult population has a gambling disorder and emphasizes population-wide interventions, binding pre-commitment, effective self-exclusion, product-safety measures, clear harm warnings, and well-resourced regulation.[1]

The National Council on Problem Gambling describes problem gambling as behavior that damages a person or family and disrupts daily life or work. Its warning signs include persistent thoughts about gambling, needing to bet more, chasing losses, irritability when cutting down, loss of control, and continuing despite negative consequences. NCPG also stresses that the size of a financial loss alone does not define harm.[2]

Arizona’s 2026 responsible-gaming training describes gambling literacy, pre-commitment, honesty and control, and warns that recognizing signs is not a clinical diagnosis. It identifies budget and time limits, breaks, accurate understanding of probability, balance with other activities, and help pathways including cognitive behavioural therapy, peer support, recovery coaches, helplines, and self-exclusion.[3]

## Editorial Integrity

The Trust Project’s eight indicators include visible best practices, journalist expertise, content labels, references, methods, local knowledge, diverse voices, and actionable feedback. Its reference and methods indicators support showing readers where claims came from and how reporting was built.[4]

The Society of Professional Journalists’ ethics code directs publishers to verify information, prefer original sources, provide context, identify sources, label advocacy and commentary, avoid distortion and plagiarism, distinguish news from advertising, explain editorial processes, and correct mistakes promptly and prominently.[5]

## Casino Regulation and Reporting

The UK Gambling Commission states that gambling advertising must be socially responsible and comply with applicable advertising codes. CasinoVerse is not a gambling operator or affiliate product, but its non-promotional presentation should continue to separate independent information from commercial persuasion and avoid misleading claims.[6]

The Nevada Gaming Control Board publishes primary-source monthly revenue reports, quarterly statistics, annual financial abstracts, active-location reports, and regulatory material. CasinoVerse market pages should describe the exact reporting period, metric, jurisdiction, and source document rather than presenting derived market figures without context.[7]

FinCEN’s casino guidance explains that certain U.S. casinos and card clubs are financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act and must maintain risk-based controls for recordkeeping and suspicious-activity reporting. Its guidance also illustrates why CasinoVerse regulatory reporting must specify jurisdiction, regulated entity type, threshold, date, and whether a statement is guidance or current law.[8]
