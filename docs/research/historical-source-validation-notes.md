## Longitudinal and Regulatory References

The American Gaming Association’s 2025 Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide describes responsible-gaming rules across 38 United States commercial gaming jurisdictions and documents common requirements including self-exclusion, advertising restrictions, account limits, treatment funding, employee training, and credit controls. This is suitable for contextualizing the evolution of responsible-play regulation rather than assigning a single historical event date.

- Source: American Gaming Association, *Responsible Gaming Regulations and Statutes Guide*, 8 July 2025: https://www.americangaming.org/resources/responsible-gaming-regulations-and-statutes-guide/
- Guide PDF: https://www.americangaming.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/AGA-2025-Responsible-Gaming-Regulations-and-Guidelines.pdf

New York State’s official Gaming Commission maintains casino, sports-wagering, video-gaming, lottery, and related revenue-report collections. The page confirms a regulator-maintained reporting structure but is not itself sufficient evidence for a year-specific event without the underlying report.

- Source: New York State Gaming Commission, *Revenue Reports*: https://gaming.ny.gov/revenue-reports

The New Jersey Casino Control Commission publishes annual reports and states that it is legally required to report casino-licensee revenues, expenses, and disbursements to the governor and legislature. The archive visibly includes annual reports for 2017 through 2024.

- Source: New Jersey Casino Control Commission, *Commission Reports*: https://www.nj.gov/casinos/about/reports/

The UNLV Gaming Research & Review Journal hosts *A Chronology of (Legal) Gaming in the U.S.* by George G. Fenich (2012). The document is useful for pre-2012 historical context, but the direct PDF extraction was incomplete and will not be used for unsupported post-2012 claims.

- Source: UNLV, *A Chronology of (Legal) Gaming in the U.S.*: https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1223&context=grrj

## 2010 Primary-Source Upgrade

Pennsylvania’s official statute records show that Chapter 13A governing table games was added on 7 January 2010. The Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board also published table-game regulation material in February 2010. These sources supersede secondary reporting as the preferred provenance for the 2010 timeline record.

- Pennsylvania General Assembly, Title 4 Chapter 13A: https://www.palegis.us/statutes/consolidated/view-statute?txtType=HTM&ttl=04&div=0&chpt=13A
- Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board, *Gaming Control Board Approves New Regulations for the Training and Play of Table Games*, 17 February 2010: https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/gaming-control-board-approves-new-regulations-training-and-play

The Gaming Control Board release confirms that table-game legislation had passed less than six weeks earlier and that the Board approved dealer-training, game-rule, surveillance, and problem-gambling recognition requirements. The final 2010 record will therefore use the regulator’s 17 February implementation milestone rather than a secondary report of legislative passage.

## 2025 Verified Regulator Milestone

On 4 February 2025, the UK Gambling Commission announced rules requiring gambling businesses from 31 October 2025 to prompt customers to set a financial limit before their first deposit, make limits easy to review, remind customers every six months, and improve disclosures concerning customer-fund protection. This replaces the imprecise 2025 candidate that reused a 2024 announcement.

- UK Gambling Commission, *New rules empowering consumers and boosting operator transparency*, 4 February 2025: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/new-rules-empowering-consumers-and-boosting-operator-transparency

## 2011–2014 Primary-Source Checks

The Massachusetts Gaming Commission confirms that Governor Deval Patrick signed Chapter 194, the Expanded Gaming Act, on 22 November 2011. The statute authorized up to three destination resort casinos and one slot facility, created the independent Commission, and included mitigation and public-health provisions.

- Massachusetts Gaming Commission, *Expanded Gaming Act*: https://massgaming.com/about/expanded-gaming-act/

The Delaware General Assembly records House Bill 333 as signed on 28 June 2012. Its official synopsis states that the Delaware Gaming Competitiveness Act authorized Internet gaming under the Delaware Lottery, including Internet versions of table games and video lottery offerings, with location, age, account-security, limit, and compulsive-gambling safeguards.

- Delaware General Assembly, *House Bill 333*: https://legis.delaware.gov/BillDetail/21016

The Massachusetts Gaming Commission confirms that on 6 November 2014 it unanimously awarded resort-casino licenses to MGM Springfield and Wynn MA, LLC and received Wynn’s $85 million license fee. This regulator record supports the 2014 timeline milestone without relying on casino-marketing material.

- Massachusetts Gaming Commission, *MassGaming officially awards resort-casino licenses*, 6 November 2014: https://massgaming.com/blog-post/video-and-images-massgaming-officially-awards-resort-casino-licenses-to-mgm-springfield-and-wynn-ma-llc-receives-85-million-license-fee/

The Nevada Legislature’s SB9 overview page remains sparse in text extraction, but its official bill identity and legislative session are verified. The archived bill PDF and Gaming Control Board industry notice will be retained together as provenance for the 2015 technology-regulation record.

## 2018–2023 Primary-Source Checks

The United Kingdom government announced on 17 May 2018 that the maximum stake on fixed-odds betting terminals would be reduced from £100 to £2 to reduce gambling-related harm. The release also recorded planned online age-verification, spending-limit, advertising, research, and treatment measures.

- UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media & Sport, *Government to cut Fixed Odds Betting Terminals maximum stake from £100 to £2*: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-to-cut-fixed-odds-betting-terminals-maximum-stake-from-100-to-2

On 15 May 2019, the UK Gambling Commission announced £4.5 million in penalty packages for four online gambling businesses after identifying anti-money-laundering and consumer-protection failures. The regulator stated that the action formed part of a broader investigation of online casino operators.

- UK Gambling Commission, *Widespread regulator action results in further £4.5m in penalty packages for online gambling sector*: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/widespread-regulator-action-results-in-further-gbp4-5m-in-penalty-packages

The Netherlands’ official Staatsblad 2021, 45 set 1 April 2021 for most of the Remote Gambling Act and related decree to enter into force, including licensing, enforcement, and modernized addiction-prevention provisions. It set 1 October 2021 for the remaining online-market and physical-venue exclusion-register provisions.

- Government of the Netherlands, Staatsblad 2021, 45: https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2021-45.html

MGM Resorts’ SEC Form 8-K states that a cybersecurity issue identified on 12 September 2023 disrupted systems at United States properties, that some personal information was obtained, and that the company estimated an approximately $100 million negative impact to third-quarter Adjusted Property EBITDAR for affected Las Vegas Strip and regional operations.

- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, MGM Resorts International Form 8-K filed 5 October 2023: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789570/000119312523251667/d461062d8k.htm

## Validation Rules

CasinoVerse will prefer statutes, regulators, government records, official institutional reports, peer-reviewed research, and public-company filings. Corporate announcements and reputable trade reporting may be used for openings or operational events when no regulator record is available. Wikipedia, promotional campaigns, unrelated products named CasinoVerse, and claims with mismatched event years will not be loaded.
