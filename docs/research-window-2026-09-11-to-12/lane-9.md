### 1. FATF Publishes New Risk Indicators for Gaming and Gambling Sectors

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) released a report titled "Risks of Gaming and Gambling," which updates its 2009 analysis of the sector. The report identifies land-based and online casinos, as well as sports betting, as having the highest exposure to money laundering. Conversely, lotteries and scratchcards are deemed to present a lower risk. The publication highlights that cash, e-wallets, mobile money, and virtual assets are the payment methods most vulnerable to abuse. The Danish Gambling Authority (Spillemyndigheden) has already confirmed the report's relevance to its licensees.

This development is highly material because FATF serves as the global watchdog for money laundering and terrorist financing. Its updated risk indicators constitute a major international regulatory framework update for the casino industry. These guidelines will directly influence compliance, security, and fraud prevention measures for operators on a global scale.

### 2. iGaming Ontario Warns of Third Parties Impersonating BetGuard Self-Exclusion Tool

iGaming Ontario has issued a public warning regarding unauthorized third parties that are actively impersonating BetGuard, the province's centralized self-exclusion tool. According to the agency, these entities are utilizing the BetGuard name to disseminate misleading advice regarding self-exclusion. In response, iGaming Ontario has directed users to rely exclusively on verified channels, such as BetGuard.ca, OLG.ca, and Connex Ontario. BetGuard is a critical responsible gambling tool that allows players to simultaneously block themselves from all regulated online gaming sites within the province.

This incident is material as it represents a direct and immediate threat to responsible gambling initiatives and the safety of vulnerable players. The impersonation of a state-backed self-exclusion mechanism by unauthorized actors constitutes a significant security and fraud issue within Ontario's regulated online casino market.

### 3. New Zealand Regulator Recovers $11.5m for Pokies Communities and Suspends Operator

New Zealand's Department of Internal Affairs (DIA) announced the recovery of $11.5 million in community pokies funding and the suspension of the operating licence for One Foundation, a class 4 gambling society, for a period of six days. This action follows a sector-wide investigation that uncovered widespread accounting failures. The probe revealed instances where funds legally mandated for community grants were instead diverted to cover society expenses. Alongside these enforcement actions, the DIA has published new financial guidance aimed at the class 4 sector.

This event is material because it represents a major enforcement action by a national regulatory body addressing the misappropriation of gambling proceeds, which is a form of financial irregularity and fraud within the sector. The significant financial recovery and the suspension of a license directly impact the operations, compliance standards, and integrity of the "pokies" (slot machine) sector in New Zealand.
